## Answer Key

### Q1. Answer: B
**Explanation**: Agentic AI is characterized by the system's ability to autonomously take actions and make decisions to achieve specified goals, rather than just responding to individual prompts. While it may use LLMs (A), that's not what makes it "agentic." Having a conversational interface (C) or processing data quickly (D) are not defining characteristics of agentic behavior.

### Q2. Answer: B
**Explanation**: Iterative refinement refers to the agent's ability to review its own outputs, identify shortcomings, and improve them through multiple iterations. This is a key capability of agentic systems. Training the model multiple times (A), updating parameters (C), and running the same prompt (D) are not what iterative refinement means in this context.

### Q3. Answer: D
**Explanation**: Random action selection is not a legitimate degree of autonomy in agentic systems. The legitimate degrees include: fully autonomous decision-making (A), human-in-the-loop with suggestions (B), and asking for permission before actions (C). Autonomy requires purposeful decision-making, not randomness.

### Q4. Answer: C
**Explanation**: Task decomposition is the process of breaking complex tasks into smaller, manageable subtasks that can be tackled sequentially or in parallel. This is a fundamental capability of agentic systems. The other options refer to different technical concepts unrelated to task decomposition.

### Q5. Answer: C
**Explanation**: One of the key benefits Andrew Ng emphasizes is that agentic workflows achieve improved accuracy through reflection and iteration. While they may sometimes have higher computational costs (opposite of A) and slower response times (opposite of B), the accuracy improvements justify these tradeoffs. They're also not necessarily simpler to implement (D).

### Q6. Answer: B
**Explanation**: Customer service and support was specifically mentioned as an application domain that benefits from agentic AI for handling complex multi-step processes like troubleshooting, information gathering, and problem resolution. While agentic AI can be applied to other domains, customer service is a prime example discussed in the course.

### Q7. Answer: B
**Explanation**: Modularity in agentic systems refers to separating concerns into specialized agents or components, each handling specific aspects of the overall task. This allows for better organization, maintenance, and scalability. The other options refer to different types of modularity not specific to agentic AI.

### Q8. Answer: C
**Explanation**: A key feature of agentic systems is their ability to reflect on errors or suboptimal outputs, identify issues, and retry with improvements. This self-correction capability distinguishes agentic AI from simpler systems that would crash (A), ignore errors (B), or require manual intervention (D).

### Q9. Answer: B
**Explanation**: The AI that generates code, tests it, debugs errors, and iterates demonstrates true agentic behavior - it takes actions, evaluates results, and autonomously works toward a goal through multiple steps. The other options (A, C, D) describe simpler, non-agentic AI systems that perform single-step tasks.

### Q10. Answer: C
**Explanation**: Autonomy and control can be balanced. Agentic systems can operate autonomously within defined boundaries and constraints set by designers. This allows for the benefits of autonomous operation while maintaining appropriate human oversight. The relationship is not binary (A, B) and control remains important (D).

### Q11. Answer: B
**Explanation**: Planning in agentic AI refers to the agent's ability to outline the steps needed to achieve a goal before executing them. This forward-thinking capability allows the agent to organize its approach and identify required resources. The other options refer to different types of planning unrelated to agent capabilities.

### Q12. Answer: B
**Explanation**: Agentic AI can handle ambiguous tasks by asking clarifying questions and making reasonable assumptions based on context. This flexibility is a key advantage over rigid systems. Refusing to proceed (A), defaulting to standard solutions (C), or computational efficiency (D) are not the primary advantages for ambiguous tasks.

### Q13. Answer: B
**Explanation**: Tool use refers to the agent's ability to call external functions, APIs, databases, search engines, calculators, or other services to accomplish tasks it couldn't do through language generation alone. This extends the agent's capabilities beyond just generating text. The other options refer to different uses of "tool."

### Q14. Answer: B
**Explanation**: Agentic AI systems are often slower than zero-shot prompting because they perform multiple iterations of refinement, verification, and self-correction to achieve higher quality results. This is an intentional tradeoff of speed for quality. The other options are not reasons for slower performance.

### Q15. Answer: B
**Explanation**: The core value proposition is trading some speed and simplicity for significantly improved reliability, quality, and capability through iterative processes. Agentic AI doesn't replace human expertise (A), make systems free (C), or eliminate prompt engineering (D), but it does enhance the quality and reliability of AI outputs through autonomous iterative refinement.
